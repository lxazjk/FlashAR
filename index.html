Abstract

Large-scale autoregressive models have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in image generation. However, their sequential raster-scan decoding relies on strictly next-token prediction, making inference prohibitively expensive. Existing acceleration methods typically either introduce entirely new generation paradigms that necessitate costly pre-training from scratch, or enable parallel generation at the expense of a training-inference gap or altered prediction objectives.

In this paper, we introduce FlashAR, a lightweight post-training adaptation framework that efficiently adapts a pre-trained raster-scan autoregressive model into a highly parallel generator based on two-way next-token prediction. We retain the original AR head as a horizontal head for row-wise prediction and introduce a complementary, lightweight vertical head for column-wise prediction. To facilitate efficient adaptation, we branch the vertical head from an intermediate layer rather than the final layer, bypassing the inherent horizontal head bias. We further employ a learnable fusion gate to dynamically combine the two predictions at each position, and propose a two-stage adaptation pipeline to reduce post-training cost.

Extensive experiments on LlamaGen and Emu3.5 show that FlashAR achieves up to a 22.9× speedup for 512×512 image generation through lightweight post-training with merely 0.05% of the original training data.

Method

Figure 2. Overview of the FlashAR framework. Initialized from a pre-trained raster-scan autoregressive model, the architecture incorporates an intermediate branching module for the vertical head and a learnable fusion gate, facilitating efficient adaptation to parallel generation.

(1) Intermediate Branching for Dual-head Decoding

Instead of introducing the vertical prediction branch at the final layer, we branch over the intermediate layers of the decoder where features remain semantically rich and less coupled to the original horizontal decoding direction. The horizontal branch utilizes the original top layers for row-wise prediction, while the vertical branch employs an independently trainable block for column-wise prediction.

(2) Learnable Fusion Gate

Since horizontal and vertical predictions capture complementary dependencies whose relative importance varies across target positions, we fuse the two predictions with a learnable fusion gate rather than simply averaging them. This context-dependent gate dynamically balances horizontal and vertical logits at each spatial position.

(3) Two-Stage Adaptation Pipeline

Stage 1 — Branch Initialization: We freeze the pre-trained backbone and the original horizontal head. Only the newly initialized vertical head and gating mechanism are trainable.

Stage 2 — Joint Fine-Tuning: The entire network is unfrozen and jointly fine-tuned to adapt to the new two-way decoding paradigm.
