Abstract

Large-scale autoregressive models have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in image generation. However, their sequential raster-scan decoding relies on strictly next-token prediction, making inference prohibitively expensive. Existing acceleration methods typically either introduce entirely new generation paradigms that necessitate costly pre-training from scratch, or enable parallel generation at the expense of a training-inference gap or altered prediction objectives.

In this paper, we introduce FlashAR, a lightweight post-training adaptation framework that efficiently adapts a pre-trained raster-scan autoregressive model into a highly parallel generator based on two-way next-token prediction. We retain the original AR head as a horizontal head for row-wise prediction and introduce a complementary, lightweight vertical head for column-wise prediction. To facilitate efficient adaptation, we branch the vertical head from an intermediate layer rather than the final layer, bypassing the inherent horizontal head bias. We further employ a learnable fusion gate to dynamically combine the two predictions at each position, and propose a two-stage adaptation pipeline to reduce post-training cost.

Extensive experiments on LlamaGen and Emu3.5 show that FlashAR achieves up to a 22.9× speedup for 512×512 image generation through lightweight post-training with merely 0.05% of the original training data.

Method

Figure 2. Overview of the FlashAR framework. Initialized from a pre-trained raster-scan autoregressive model, the architecture incorporates an intermediate branching module for the vertical head and a learnable fusion gate, facilitating efficient adaptation to parallel generation.

Standard raster-scan AR models factorize image generation as strict left-to-right, top-to-bottom next-token prediction, requiring H \times W sequential decoding steps. FlashAR reformulates this into a diagonal-step factorization: tokens on the same anti-diagonal (i.e., positions where p + q = t) depend only on previously decoded tokens and can therefore be generated in parallel, reducing serial steps from H \times W to H + W - 1.

(1) Intermediate Branching for Dual-head Decoding

To realize this two-way prediction, we retain the original AR head as a horizontal head for row-wise prediction and introduce a lightweight vertical head for column-wise prediction. A key challenge is that the pre-trained model’s top-layer representations are inherently biased toward the original horizontal raster-scan objective. Our linear probing analysis confirms that deeper layers become increasingly specialized to horizontal prediction, retaining less cross-axis information. Therefore, instead of attaching the vertical head at the final layer, we branch it from an intermediate upper layer where features remain semantically rich and less direction-specific. This branching design also improves runtime efficiency: the horizontal and vertical blocks execute concurrently on top of the shared trunk, maintaining the original critical-path depth.

(2) Learnable Fusion Gate

The horizontal and vertical heads provide two complementary predictions for each image token. While simple averaging (as used in NAR) assumes isotropic contribution from both directions, natural images often exhibit significant anisotropy—for instance, along a sharp horizontal edge, the vertical predecessor may provide more reliable structural continuity. Moreover, when the two heads yield conflicting logit distributions, direct averaging acts as a low-pass filter, causing blurred textures.

We therefore introduce a learnable fusion gate that computes a context-dependent weight from the two predecessor hidden states via an MLP with sigmoid activation. The fused logit at each position is a weighted combination of the horizontal and vertical predictions, allowing the model to adaptively exploit their complementarity. For boundary positions, only the available directional prediction is used: the first row uses horizontal logits, and the first column uses vertical logits.

(3) Two-Stage Adaptation Pipeline

Stage 1 — Branch Initialization: We freeze the pre-trained backbone and the original horizontal head. Only the newly initialized vertical head and gating mechanism are trainable. This frozen-backbone strategy prevents catastrophic forgetting and ensures rapid, stable convergence for the new components.

Stage 2 — Joint Fine-Tuning: The entire network is unfrozen and jointly fine-tuned end-to-end. The backbone, horizontal head, vertical head, and gating module are jointly optimized, harmonizing orthogonal spatial representations for the new diagonal-parallel decoding paradigm. The backbone learning rate is scaled by 0.2\times relative to the heads to preserve pre-trained knowledge.

(4) Hardware-Aware Inference Pipeline

To translate theoretical parallelism into wall-clock speedups, we implement a hardware-aware inference pipeline. We leverage FlexAttention to dynamically compile sparse 2D diagonal-step masks on the fly, entirely bypassing the materialization of the full attention matrix. After sampling each diagonal, all tokens are appended to branch-specific KV caches in a single batched operation. We similarly batch the conditional and unconditional forward passes for classifier-free guidance, amortizing kernel launch overheads and maximizing GPU arithmetic intensity.
